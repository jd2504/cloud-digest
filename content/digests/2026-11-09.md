## Architecture & design patterns

*Search found no clearly dated November papers or engineering posts. The items below are older than the window and included as recently notable; treat dates accordingly.*

### From Inference Engine to Inference Control Plane (arXiv 2609.23130, ~September 2026)

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23130). The paper traces how LLM serving is shifting from a single engine such as vLLM to a control plane (e.g., llm-d) that routes and schedules requests across many engines and GPUs. The idea is to treat serving as a distributed-systems problem with its own scheduling and placement layer. Apply it when you run multi-replica LLM inference and need cache-aware routing and SLO-based scheduling instead of plain load balancing.

### Optimizing Cloud Deployment: Blending IaaS and FaaS for Microservices (arXiv 2606.11824, June 2026)

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11824). The authors propose using service-level performance metrics to decide which microservices should move from VM-based hosting to serverless functions, producing a hybrid deployment. Apply it when migrating an existing microservice estate and you want evidence-based selection of spiky, stateless candidates for FaaS.

### Cost as a resilience decision: the "Cost of Failure" lens (FinOps X 2026 material)

Source: [Advania community article](https://community.advania.co.uk/kb/articles/37-cost-and-resilience-in-the-cloud-navigating-the-trade-offs). The piece argues resilience investment should scale to the business impact of failure, comparing architectures by expected financial exposure (cost of failure times probability) rather than applying uniform standards. Apply it when deciding which workloads justify multi-region or active-active designs and which can accept simpler recovery. This is a vendor-community article, not a primary source.

## Takeaway

AI demand is now the organizing force in cloud: it is driving hyperscaler growth, new accelerator launches, cross-cloud data access, and a rethink of serving architectures as control planes, so architects should plan for portable data and inference layers rather than single-provider stacks.
