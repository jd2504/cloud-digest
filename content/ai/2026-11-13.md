*This is the first issue of the AI Digest. It covers Tuesday, November 10 through Friday morning, November 13, and also includes Anthropic's November 9 model launch, which fell just before the window and has not been covered here before.*

## Model & platform announcements

### Anthropic

**Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 launch at a lower price**
- Status: GA for Fable 5.2 (`claude-fable-5-2`). Mythos 5.2 (`claude-mythos-5-2`) is limited to organizations approved through Anthropic's Cyber Verification and Life Sciences Verification programs. Launched November 9.
- Availability and pricing: $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, down from $10/$50 for the 5.1 models. A fast mode for Fable 5.2 costs $16/$80. Both have a 1M-token context window and 128K max output. They are available on the Claude API, Amazon Bedrock, Claude Platform on AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry. Both require 30-day data retention and aren't available under zero data retention without authorization. Computer use needs the new `computer_toolset_20260801` toolset.
- Why it matters: Anthropic describes Fable and Mythos as the same underlying model, with Fable carrying stronger cyber and bio safeguards. The company reports gains over Opus 5.5 on FrontierSWE v2 (69.9% vs 62.3%) and AutomationBench (51.4% vs 42.5%). The 20% price cut narrows the gap to Opus 5.5 ($4/$20), but the retention rule may exclude some regulated workloads. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2); [Claude Platform release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview))

**Self-hosted Claude Managed Agents**
- Status: launched November 12. Access is by request through Anthropic's account team.
- Why it matters: teams that liked the managed agent loop but couldn't send tool execution or data through Anthropic's infrastructure now have a self-hosted option, which matters for data-residency and network-isolation reviews. ([Claude Platform release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview))

**Reminder: two retirements on November 30**
- Claude Sonnet 4.5 (`claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`) retires from the Claude API on November 30; the named replacement is Sonnet 5.5. Fast mode for Opus 4.8 is also removed that day; the replacement is Opus 5.5. Both were announced earlier and are listed here because the deadline is close. ([Claude Platform release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview))

### Google

**Gemini 4 Hydrogen: Argon-class scores at a mid-tier price**
- Status: released November 12 and rolling out to everyone. VentureBeat reports that Google Cloud lists it as generally available. API ID `gemini-4-hydrogen`.
- Availability and pricing: $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, flat across the 1M-token context window, on the Gemini API (AI Studio), Gemini Enterprise and Google Cloud's Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform. VentureBeat reports $0.075 for cached input and half price for Batch and Flex. Thinking levels are low, medium, high (the default) and a new xhigh.
- Why it matters: Google reports an Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index score of 51 at high thinking, against 50 for its flagship Gemini 4 Argon, which costs $4/$20 from December 1. VentureBeat notes Hydrogen costs twice what Gemini 3.8 Flash costs today, though it matches Flash's scheduled 2027 rate. Migration needs attention: the default thinking level is higher than on Flash, the old `thinking_budget` parameter and MINIMAL setting are rejected, and explicit caching isn't available at launch. ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/); [Google Cloud docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/gemini/4-hydrogen); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/google-launches-gemini-4-hydrogen-a-faster-smarter-pricier-flash-successor))

**Gemini Decisions API and Gemini Decision 1**
- Status: launched November 11. Google's post doesn't say whether it is in preview or GA. Model `gemini-decision-1`.
- Availability and pricing: $0.10 per million input tokens and no charge for output, on the Gemini API and AI Studio (with a free prototyping tier). Google says Google Cloud availability is coming.
- Why it matters: the endpoint answers closed questions (yes/no, multiple choice, rubric scores) over text, images, audio, video and PDFs. It takes up to 1,000 questions per request and returns a 0–1 confidence score with each answer, in 150–250 ms end to end according to Google. That makes it a cheap classifier, router or guardrail check. SiliconANGLE notes it follows TypeSafe's Jev and an OpenAI Decisions API in October. Google's claim that it beat both on 7 of 9 benchmarks is its own measurement. ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/11/google-enters-the-decision-model-market-with-gemini-decisions-api/))

**Reminder: Gemini audio previews shut down November 17**
- `gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview` and `gemini-2.5-flash-native-audio-preview-12-2025` are replaced by `gemini-3.8-live`. `gemini-3.1-flash-tts-preview`, `gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts` and `gemini-2.5-pro-preview-tts` are replaced by `gemini-3.8-flash-tts` or `gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts`. These were announced earlier. Google says the listed dates are the earliest possible shutdowns. ([Gemini API deprecations](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations))

### OpenAI

**gpt-image-2 scheduled for shutdown on February 10, 2027**
- Status: retiring. Announced November 10 for `gpt-image-2` and `gpt-image-2-2026-04-21`. The replacements are `gpt-image-2.6-sunburst` or `gpt-image-2.6-flare`, which shipped on November 4 with SVG and animated-GIF output.
- Why it matters: any image pipeline pinned to gpt-image-2 has about three months to migrate and re-test. Note also that `/v1/prompts`, the Evals dashboard and API, and Agent Builder shut down on November 30 (announced in June), and `gpt-5`, `gpt-5-mini`, `gpt-5-nano`, `o3` and `o3-pro` shut down on December 11. ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations); [OpenAI API changelog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog))

### Model platforms

**Amazon Bedrock: Claude Mythos 5.2 in AWS GovCloud, and new data-retention rules for Fable 5.2**
- Status: Mythos 5.2 is available to allow-listed organizations in AWS GovCloud (US) Regions (announced November 9). Fable 5.2 is on Bedrock as `global.anthropic.claude-fable-5-2`, through US Geo and Global cross-Region inference.
- Why it matters: AWS says Fable 5.2 is a "Covered Model" that requires up to 30 days of retention and human safety review, done by Amazon staff inside the AWS boundary. You must set the `aws_review` data retention mode before invoking. AWS says eligible customers can keep zero data retention for internal use until at least June 30, 2027. Applications must handle declined requests themselves; AWS suggests retrying cyber refusals on Opus 4.8 and biology refusals on Opus 5. Plan for that fallback path before swapping model IDs. ([AWS What's New](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/bedrock-claude-mythos-5-2-govcloud/); [AWS Machine Learning Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/introducing-claude-fable-5-2-on-aws/))

### Protocols & open source

**MCP moves to a six-month specification release cycle**
- Status: announced November 11. New spec versions will land on the last Tuesday of March and September, starting with `2027-03-30`. Each release follows a fixed schedule: a soft freeze about 2.5 months out, Tier 1 SDK betas about 1.5 months out, and a hard freeze one month out.
- Why it matters: teams running MCP servers and clients can now schedule upgrade and test windows, and the version-date scheme isn't changing. ([MCP Blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/mcp-release-cycle/))

**Draft MCP extension for file uploads and downloads**
- Status: experimental early draft, published November 10, with no reference implementation yet. Extension ID `io.modelcontextprotocol/files`, for protocol revision `2026-07-28` and later.
- Why it matters: moving files between agents and tools is a common workaround today. The draft adds an `upload_required` result type and download targets with an explicit credentials flag, and requires clients to refuse redirects. Watch it if you build MCP servers that handle documents. ([MCP Blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/exploring-file-uploads-and-downloads-in-mcp/))

## Industry & policy news

**Anthropic sets its IPO price range at $70–$78 a share.** Anthropic launched its roadshow on November 10 for 825 million Class A shares, to list on Nasdaq as "ANT". TechCrunch, citing the updated S-1, reports an implied valuation of about $1.4 trillion at the top of the range, run-rate revenue of $81 billion at the end of October, and trading expected around November 19 (a date CNBC first reported). For buyers, a public Anthropic means quarterly disclosure of the pricing, capacity and compute commitments behind a major model supplier. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-sets-ipo-price-range-of-70-to-78-per-share-to-reach-an-implied-valuation-of-1-404-trillion/))

**New York opens registration for frontier developers under the RAISE Act.** The state opened its registration portal on November 10, and large frontier developers, including OpenAI and Anthropic, must register by January 1, 2027, according to TechCrunch. From January 2027, the state says, covered developers must publish safety frameworks, report critical safety incidents to the DIGIT office within 72 hours and file quarterly catastrophic-risk assessments. The obligations fall on model makers, but enterprise buyers will be able to read the frameworks and incident disclosures. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/openai-anthropic-face-new-oversight-as-new-yorks-ai-safety-law-takes-effect/); [Governor of New York](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/ai-safety-governor-hochul-announces-next-steps-regulate-major-ai-developers-and-protect-new))

**Mercor raises $750 million at a $20 billion valuation to sell agent evaluation to enterprises.** The Series D, announced November 10, includes Nvidia, Salesforce and Zoom. Mercor says it will spend the money on research and evaluation infrastructure, including more training environments. It is pitching custom benchmarks and post-training for agents built for each enterprise, a sign that evaluation is becoming a product category you can buy rather than an internal chore. ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/10/mercor-raises-750m-at-20b-valuation-to-take-ai-agent-evaluation-to-enterprises))

**NVIDIA contributes OpenShell, an agent-governance runtime, to the CNCF.** OpenShell joined the CNCF as a Sandbox project on November 10. It enforces policy limits on autonomous agents from the infrastructure layer rather than inside the agent, uses Cedar as its policy language, and keeps credentials scoped and hidden from agents. Red Hat, Docker and Canonical are core contributors alongside NVIDIA. It gives platform teams a vendor-neutral place to standardize agent sandboxing on Kubernetes. ([CNCF](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/11/10/welcoming-openshell-to-the-cncf-open-governance-to-secure-agentic-ai/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/11/prompt-injection-attacks-drive-red-hat-nvidia-s-openshell-kubeconna/))

## Engineering patterns & research

### A governed, observable loop architecture for enterprise agents
Source: [AWS Architecture Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/architecture/inside-an-enterprise-ai-agent-a-governed-observable-loop-architecture/) (November 12). AWS lays out a five-layer reference design: an API gateway that handles identity and policy, an agent runtime that runs understand, plan, retrieve, reason and act phases with observation throughout, a knowledge and state tier, shared platform services, and a trust-and-control layer that cuts across all of them. The concrete advice is to cap loop iterations, set timeouts and token budgets, hand off to a human when the agent isn't confident, and launch agents read-only before widening their permissions. Use it as a checklist when an agent moves from prototype to a production system with real write access.

### Benchmarking code-retrieval setups for coding agents (JioHotstar)
Source: [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/jiohotstar-codingagent-retrieval/) (November 11), reporting a JioHotstar engineering post. The team compared six setups (a plain filesystem baseline, Sourcebot, Graphify, CocoIndex Code, Repowise and an in-house RAG pipeline) on subsets of SWE-bench and CodeScaleBench. Structural code search beat the baseline on harder tasks at lower cost. Retrieval added overhead on simple tasks, and semantic indexing helped mainly when requirements were vague. The subsets were small, so read the results as directional. Apply it before adding a vector index to a coding agent: keep filesystem tools as the baseline, and measure tool-call traces as well as accuracy.

### Decision models as a cheap first-pass evaluator
Source: [AWS Machine Learning Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/use-decision-models-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-evaluations/) (November 4, just before this window; included because Google's Decisions API launch makes it timely). The post plugs two decision models, TypeSafe's hosted Jev and the open-source Strands Decider 2B, into Bedrock AgentCore Evaluations. It recommends a cascade: the decision model scores every agent turn in hundreds of milliseconds, and only low-confidence turns go to a full LLM judge, which takes 2–10 seconds. It also gives a validation procedure against human majority labels. The cited JEV-as-a-Judge paper is a preprint. Apply it when LLM-as-judge costs or latency stop you from evaluating every production turn.

## Takeaway

The center of gravity this week is price-performance. Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen posts flagship-level index scores at $1.50/$7.50, Anthropic cut its top model's price by 20%, and closed-question decision models now cost cents per million tokens. For architects, model choice is becoming a routing decision: send each task to the cheapest tier that clears your own evaluations. At the same time, deprecation calendars are getting crowded (November 17, November 30, December 11), so pinned model IDs need an owner and a migration plan.
