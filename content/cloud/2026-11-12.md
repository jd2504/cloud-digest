## Industry news

*Coverage note: this run could search the web but could not open article pages directly, so the items below rest on search-result excerpts from the linked outlets and primary sources. Details that appeared in only one non-primary source have been left out or flagged.*

**CoreWeave's revenue jumps 168% and it raises its 2026 outlook.** The GPU cloud reported third-quarter revenue of about $3.66 billion on November 10, ahead of the roughly $3.55 billion analysts expected, and lifted its full-year revenue guidance; shares swung after hours. Going into the print, CNBC framed it as a test of a debt-funded build-out, since guided interest expense was well above adjusted operating income, so the beat matters less than whether financing costs keep pace. ([CNBC — after-hours movers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/stock-market-movers-live-updates.html); [CNBC — earnings preview](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-earnings-preview-debt-capex.html))

**Microsoft lets a 2023 compute deal with Oracle lapse as it trims its supplier list.** Microsoft declined to renew a 2023 capacity contract with Oracle that covered older Nvidia hardware, according to DCD, which credits Bloomberg with the original report; newer contracts between the two appear unaffected, and Oracle says demand for its infrastructure remains strong. CNBC separately reports that Microsoft is cutting the number of neoclouds it relies on and leaning on its own data centers, a signal that hyperscaler overflow demand for third-party GPU capacity is no longer a given. ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-winds-down-2023-compute-deal-with-oracle/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/microsoft-moves-to-consolidate-its-relationships-with-neoclouds-in-ai.html))

**SWI Group launches SWI Compute, a new European AI cloud, with about $1 billion of Nvidia orders.** The Amsterdam-listed power-and-sites company is spinning up an AI cloud unit whose first site in Cambridgeshire, UK, starts at 30 MW of Vera Rubin systems and is planned to grow toward roughly 500 MW, with the first capacity expected by the end of 2027. It is another case of a company with secured grid access turning that scarce asset into a GPU cloud. ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/swi-launches-ai-cloud-unit-swi-compute/))

**Google open-sources TPU Sync for moving KV cache on TPUs.** The Apache 2.0 library moves KV cache (and, experimentally, model weights) between TPU memory, host DRAM and the network, and plugs into vLLM and SGLang as a transfer backend for prefill/decode disaggregation and cache offloading. Google's reported time-to-first-token gains are vendor figures, but the release shows disaggregated inference becoming standard plumbing on non-Nvidia accelerators too. ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/11/google-tpu-sync); [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/google-tpu-sync-inference/))

**AWS rolls out a new AgentCore Runtime for hosted AI agents.** AWS's November 9 weekly roundup leads with a reworked runtime for Bedrock AgentCore that it describes as elastic and optimized for consistently fast starts, adoptable on existing deployments largely by switching one setting; the same roundup lists DynamoDB bulk put items. Start-up latency and elasticity for agent sessions are becoming a competitive axis among managed agent platforms. ([AWS News Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/aws-weekly-roundup-agentcore-runtime-v2-amazon-dynamodb-bulk-put-items-and-more-november-9-2026/))

**KubeCon North America wraps in Salt Lake City with AI inference at the center.** The November 9–12 event added a dedicated AI Inference + Agentic track (GPU scheduling, model serving with vLLM and KServe, observability), and CNCF used it to launch a Certified Kubernetes Network Engineer exam aimed at AI-era networking. Show-floor analysis from SiliconANGLE argued that many young AI infrastructure clouds will be sorted by their software operations skills rather than GPU access. ([CNCF — CKNE](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/11/10/cncf-launches-certified-kubernetes-network-engineer-certification-ckne-to-advance-production-ready-networking-skills-for-ai-era/); [CNCF — schedule](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/08/10/cncf-reveals-kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america-2026-schedule-adds-new-ai-inference-agentic-track/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/11/ai-infrastructure-clouds-face-shakeout-over-software-skills-kubeconna/))

## Architecture & design patterns

### Choosing QuePaxa: leaderless consensus for a global control plane
Source: [Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/choosing-quepaxa) (November 11), building on the July [Meerkat introduction](https://blog.cloudflare.com/meerkat-introduction/). Cloudflare explains why its experimental Meerkat consensus service uses QuePaxa, a 2023 SOSP algorithm from EPFL, rather than Raft or Multi-Paxos. In QuePaxa any replica can propose at any time and progress never waits on a leader-election timeout, which Cloudflare says addresses incidents it has had when leaders became unreachable over wide-area links; a one-round-trip fast path keeps the normal case competitive. The cost is that every write still waits on a cross-region quorum. Apply it to globally replicated control-plane state (leases, configuration, metadata) where availability under partial failure matters more than single-digit-millisecond writes.

### Off the hot path: how Cloudflare's D1 control plane scales to millions of databases
Source: [Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/d1-control-plane/); coverage by [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/cloudflare-d1-control-plane/). Each D1 database is a single SQLite Durable Object; metadata lives in a central Postgres and routing data in globally replicated Workers KV, so queries never touch the central database. Each object heartbeats about every 30 seconds (with jitter) to reconcile its state, which lets failed creates and deletes repair themselves, and usage accounting is queued, batched and maintained incrementally. Apply it when building a multi-tenant control plane with very many small tenants: keep single-region components out of the request path and rely on idempotent operations plus periodic reconciliation instead of distributed transactions.

### LinkedIn replaces Argo CD with an in-house GitOps engine
Source: [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/11/kubernetes-argocd-linkedin). InfoQ reports that LinkedIn built its own GitOps deployment engine for application rollouts across more than 250 Kubernetes clusters, and that deployment-infrastructure failures fell from 925 to 63 even as deployment volume grew 3.7 times; Argo CD stays in place for cluster add-ons and system services. The lesson is less "abandon Argo" than "split fleet-scale app delivery from cluster bootstrapping when one tool's sync model becomes the bottleneck." Consider it when a platform team runs hundreds of clusters and general-purpose GitOps controllers are the main source of deployment failures.

### Cost-efficient multi-agent LLM batch workflows with built-in resilience (Walmart, QCon talk)
Source: [InfoQ presentation](https://www.infoq.com/presentations/scaling-multi-agent-llm-batch/) (published November 10). A Walmart Global Tech engineer describes running multi-agent LLM pipelines as nightly offline batch jobs that produce inventory-accuracy insights across thousands of stores, scheduling them in off-peak hours to control cost. The talk covers how agent coordination breaks under load and treats grounding, output validation and human review in the final layer as the main safeguards. Apply it when an agent workflow does not need to be interactive: batching it gives you cheaper capacity, room for retries and a natural checkpoint for validation.

## Takeaway

This window's news shows AI infrastructure economics starting to sort winners from losers: Microsoft is narrowing its list of outside capacity suppliers, CoreWeave's strong growth is being judged against its borrowing, and new entrants like SWI are leaning on the one thing that is truly scarce, power. At the same time, the engineering edge is shifting into software, such as KV-cache movement for disaggregated inference, faster agent runtimes, and leaderless or reconciliation-based control planes. Architects should assume GPU capacity will become a commodity and invest in the layers that move state and keep systems running under failure.
